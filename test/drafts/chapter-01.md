The lunch boxes arrived before nine.

The shelf boy wiped each lid with a dry cloth. Monday was clean. Tuesday was clean. By Friday, the little notebook had five neat ticks in a row.

"Then they are always careful," said the new clerk.

The older clerk shook the rain from his sleeve. "Put the soup boxes on the top shelf today."

The new clerk looked at the leaking window. The top shelf shook whenever the back door closed. It was a poor place for soup.

At noon the door banged twelve times. Couriers came in wet. Boxes knocked against boxes. The rice stayed put. The soup lids held.

The new clerk smiled and reached for a red stamp.

"Not that one," said the older clerk. "Use the blue one."

The blue stamp said, Good for now.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Induction Problem; Falsifiability; Corroboration
- Discipline or School: Philosophy of science, especially Hume and Popper.
- Core Definition: The induction problem says repeated observations cannot logically prove a universal rule. Falsifiability says a claim becomes testable when it rules out possible failures and risks being wrong. Corroboration is the provisional standing a claim earns after surviving a severe test, without becoming proven forever.
- Story Mapping: The five neat ticks represent repeated confirming observations, which still cannot prove that the supplier is always careful. The top shelf on a rainy day creates a risky condition under which the supplier's promise could fail. The blue stamp, "Good for now," represents corroboration: earned trust after a severe test, not final proof.
