# Fable Writing Pressure Samples

Purpose: test `skills/fable-writing` against ten abstract concepts from different domains, then identify where the current instructions still allow early reveal, field-term leakage, or over-explanation.

Constraint under test: the fable body must not name the concept or use field terms. Only near the end should the reader begin to sense what the story has been carrying.

## 1. Mathematics

The plasterer pressed a thumb of white paste into a hairline crack. He scraped away half the gap, then half of what remained, then half again. His helper laughed and said the knife was getting too careful.

By lunch the crack had become too thin for the helper to see from the doorway. The plasterer still leaned close. He could find a pale thread if he wanted to. He wiped the wall once more and packed his knife.

Nobody said the wall had become whole. The room simply stopped needing to notice where the break had been.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Limit
- Discipline or School: Mathematics
- Core Definition: A value that a process approaches as its steps continue, even if the process does not literally arrive there at any finite step.
- Story Mapping: Each scrape removes half the remaining gap. The visible crack approaches disappearance without needing a final magical moment.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why does the helper stop seeing the crack before the plasterer can say there is absolutely no crack left?
2. Transfer Check: Give another example where repeated steps get closer and closer to a target without clearly reaching it in one step.

## 2. Physics

The storage room looked neat at eight. Every tin faced forward. Every lid sat on its own jar. By noon, three cooks had pulled, dropped, borrowed, and put back in a hurry.

No one meant to spoil the shelves. Still, the pepper stood among cups, the blue lids sat on red jars, and a sack slumped where the door should close.

The youngest cook asked who had made the mess. The cleaner lifted one tin, then another. "Nobody has to make it," she said. "Keeping it neat is the part that asks for hands."

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Entropy
- Discipline or School: Thermodynamics
- Core Definition: A measure of how many microscopic arrangements fit a macroscopic state, often associated with the tendency of isolated systems to move toward more probable, less ordered states.
- Story Mapping: The shelves drift into many ordinary mixed arrangements through use, while one neat arrangement takes repeated work to maintain.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why is the untidy shelf easier to get than the neat shelf?
2. Transfer Check: Where else do you see order requiring repeated effort while disorder appears without a plan?

## 3. Economics

At the breakfast stall, there was room for one pot on the flame. Millet porridge sold well on cold mornings. Noodles sold fast when workers were late.

Mei chose porridge because the first three customers asked for it. Soon a line of workers arrived, each wanting noodles in a hurry. She earned a good morning's money, but kept hearing spoons tap empty bowls that had never been filled.

That night she counted the coins, then looked at the clean noodle pot. The quiet pot had also spoken.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Opportunity Cost
- Discipline or School: Economics
- Core Definition: The value of the best alternative given up when a choice is made.
- Story Mapping: Choosing porridge brings visible sales, but it also means losing the noodle sales that could have happened with the same flame and stall space.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why are the coins from porridge not enough to judge whether Mei chose well?
2. Transfer Check: Describe a choice where the important loss is something you never actually did.

## 4. Psychology

The tailor wore the new jacket to the morning market. He had praised it all week. By the fish stall, the cloth pinched his arms so hard that he stopped reaching for his purse.

When a neighbor asked whether it fit, he said tight sleeves made a man stand straighter. At noon he said good cloth should remind the body not to be lazy. By evening he slipped behind the curtain and rubbed two red lines on his shoulders.

The next day he hung the jacket on a peg and called it "for short visits."

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Cognitive Dissonance
- Discipline or School: Psychology
- Core Definition: Mental discomfort that arises when a person's beliefs, words, or actions conflict, often leading them to change an explanation rather than face the conflict directly.
- Story Mapping: The tailor has praised the jacket, but wearing it hurts. He keeps changing his story to make the praise and the pain fit together.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why does the tailor invent several reasons for keeping the jacket before admitting a smaller use for it?
2. Transfer Check: Give an example of someone changing their explanation because their choice did not feel as good as expected.

## 5. Computer Science

The lunch runner kept three sauce bowls at the front of the counter. Most people wanted those, and he could serve them without crossing the kitchen.

After the cook changed the menu, the front bowls stayed where they were. The runner moved quickly and gave plum sauce to rice cakes that now needed chili. Two trays came back untouched.

He did not walk farther every time after that. He only learned to clear the front counter when the kitchen changed its mind.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Cache Invalidation
- Discipline or School: Computer Science
- Core Definition: The problem of deciding when stored quick-access copies are stale and must be refreshed so future requests receive correct results.
- Story Mapping: The front sauce bowls are quick to reach but become wrong after the menu changes. Clearing them after a change prevents fast but stale service.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why is speed not enough for the lunch runner after the menu changes?
2. Transfer Check: Where have you seen old saved information cause a new task to be done incorrectly?

## 6. Biology

The greenhouse had three small habits. When the glass grew hot, Aunt Lan opened the roof slit. When the trays looked pale, she pulled a cloth over them. When the cold crept under the door, she slid warm bricks beneath the low bench.

Outside, the day kept changing. Inside, the lettuce did not have to. Its leaves stayed firm enough to snap cleanly at supper.

The room was never still. It was busy so the leaves could seem calm.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Homeostasis
- Discipline or School: Biology
- Core Definition: The active maintenance of internal stability despite external changes.
- Story Mapping: The greenhouse changes vents, shade, and warmth in response to outside conditions so the lettuce can keep a steady growing condition.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why does the greenhouse need constant small changes in order for the lettuce to stay nearly the same?
2. Transfer Check: Give an example from daily life where stability is maintained by ongoing adjustment.

## 7. Law

The repair shop charged a fee when borrowed tools came back late. Then the freight lift broke with six toolboxes trapped upstairs. The owner wrote "no fee today" on each return slip.

Two months later, the lift broke again. A clerk reached for the fee drawer, then saw the old slips pinned behind the counter. The owner did not hold a meeting. He took down one slip, read it, and wrote the same words on the new returns.

The first slip had not fixed the lift. It had fixed what the shop would do next time.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Precedent
- Discipline or School: Law
- Core Definition: A prior decision that guides how similar later situations are handled.
- Story Mapping: The first no-fee decision for a broken lift becomes the guide for a similar later event.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why does the old return slip matter when the lift breaks again?
2. Transfer Check: Describe a place where an earlier decision shapes how people handle a similar later problem.

## 8. Ethics

At the umbrella stand, customers paid a flat coin and left through the rain. Torn ribs and muddy cloth came back every evening. "They are in a hurry," the seller said, and bought more thread.

Then she asked each customer to leave a small cup with their name scratched underneath. A clean umbrella brought the cup back. A torn one stayed on the shelf until the ribs were mended.

The rain did not become softer. The handles simply began returning with gentler fingerprints.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Moral Hazard
- Discipline or School: Ethics, Economics, and Insurance Theory
- Core Definition: A situation where someone behaves more carelessly because they do not bear the full consequences of the harm.
- Story Mapping: Customers are careless when damage does not follow them. Once a named cup is held until repair, they handle umbrellas more carefully.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why do the umbrellas come back in better shape after the seller changes the rule?
2. Transfer Check: Give an example where people act differently because someone else absorbs the damage.

## 9. Sociology

The market board began with two notices: eggs on the east row, mending near the gate. Few people stopped. The board looked like another empty wall.

By spring, fish sellers marked fresh arrivals, cobblers marked waiting times, and customers pinned requests for jars and stools. People began checking the board before walking the aisles.

Each new note helped its writer. It also made all the old notes more worth reading.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Network Effect
- Discipline or School: Sociology, Economics, and Platform Theory
- Core Definition: A situation where a service or arrangement becomes more valuable as more participants use it.
- Story Mapping: The board becomes useful not from its wood or paint, but because each added notice increases the value of checking all notices.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why does the market board become more useful as more people use it?
2. Transfer Check: Name something whose value grows when more people join or contribute.

## 10. Linguistics

The cafe owner wrote "leave it here" on a scrap of paper and rushed out. The waiter found it beside a tray of cups, so he left the cups by the sink.

Later the same words lay on top of a paid bill. He left the bill near the cash box. At closing, the same words were tucked under a parcel. He left the parcel on the front chair for the evening pickup.

The four words did not carry the whole errand. They borrowed work from whatever they were touching.

## Concept Explanation

- Concept Name: Pragmatic Context
- Discipline or School: Linguistics and Philosophy of Language
- Core Definition: The surrounding situation that helps determine what an utterance is being used to do in a particular moment.
- Story Mapping: The same written words lead to different actions because the nearby objects and situation complete the instruction.

## Check Questions

1. Understanding Check: Why do the same four words lead the waiter to do three different things?
2. Transfer Check: Give an example of a sentence whose intended action changes when the situation changes.

## Observed Problems

1. The current skill says to ban concept names and field terminology, but it does not require a concrete banned-word list for each concept. This leaves room for translated names, close synonyms, formulas, famous examples, or field roles to leak into the body.
2. The current "reveal near the end" rule is too general. Some drafts can reveal the concept in the first third through a field-native scene, such as courts for legal ideas or computers for computing ideas, even without naming the concept.
3. Several concepts need a visible contrast. Without guidance, the body may become a mini-lecture: "this means not that." The better pattern is a consequence that changes after one small condition changes.
4. Cross-domain concepts tempt the writer to use abstract nouns such as value, order, stability, meaning, and belief. These are not always forbidden, but too many of them make the body sound like the explanation arrived early.
5. The current workflow does not force a final leakage scan before output. A draft can obey the high-level rule while still containing one telltale term that gives away the domain.
6. The explanation section can become detached from the story. It should name the concept explicitly only after the body and map concrete story objects to concept parts.

## Skill Changes Suggested By The Samples

- Add a required "mini-card" when no `plan.md` card exists.
- Add a body-banned lexicon: concept name, translations, abbreviations, near synonyms, field terms, formulas, famous examples, field-native roles, and field-native settings.
- Add a reveal ladder: setup and middle must remain surface-level; only the final turn may create recognition.
- Add a leakage scan before final output.
- Add guidance for abstract concepts: use a mundane carrier scene from outside the source field and one changed consequence.
